Esca, also known as Black Measles, is a complex fungal disease affecting grapevines. As with any plant disease, it is important to consult with local agricultural experts, such as a viticulturist or plant pathologist, to determine the most suitable and effective treatment options for your specific vineyard and region. They will have the most up-to-date information on management practices tailored to your area. However, here are some commonly used treatments for Esca:

Fungicide/Bactericide Applications: Several fungicides and bactericides are used to manage Esca. Examples include thiophanate-methyl, fosetyl-aluminum, and phosphorous acid-based products. These treatments are typically applied preventively to protect healthy vines from infection or as curative measures on infected vines during the dormant season.

Trunk Renewal: Trunk renewal is a cultural practice that involves surgically removing the infected wood from the grapevine trunk and replacing it with healthy material. This technique is often employed for young vines or vines in the early stages of infection. It helps reduce disease progression and allows the vine to recover.

Canopy Management: Proper canopy management, including pruning and leaf removal, can help improve airflow and reduce humidity within the vineyard. This can discourage the development and spread of Esca. Opening up the canopy allows sunlight penetration, which can inhibit fungal growth.

Sanitation Practices: Removing and destroying infected plant material, including pruning debris and diseased vines, can help reduce the inoculum source and limit disease spread. Regularly cleaning and sanitizing pruning tools between cuts can also prevent disease transmission.

Soil Management: Maintaining balanced soil nutrition and optimizing vine health through appropriate soil amendments can contribute to the overall resilience of grapevines against diseases like Esca.

It is crucial to remember that Esca management often requires an integrated approach combining multiple strategies. Each vineyard may have unique considerations, and professional advice tailored to your specific situation is recommended for effective control of Esca.
